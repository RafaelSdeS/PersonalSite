Kernel-size study for Winograd hardware ↗

Research report (PDF, PT) ↗

Hardware-aware neural networks: how restricting CNN kernels to the 2×2 and 3×3 sizes that Winograd hardware handles well affects accuracy, efficiency and INT8 quantization. Advised by Prof. Mateus Grellert, of UFRGS’s Microelectronics Group.

Contribution & learnings

How I contributeI run the kernel-size study end to end on PCAD, UFRGS’s HPC cluster, through Slurm: 152 runs in the main study, plus robustness and hybrid rounds on Tiny ImageNet and PASCAL VOC, each trained in FP32, then quantization-aware, then converted to INT8.

What I’m learningMeasure the seed noise before trusting a difference: between two single runs, the seed alone moves the gap by 0.7 to 1 point, so anything inside that band is a tie, and the best of 24 configurations had to be rerun with two more seeds before I trusted it. Beyond the technical side: keeping a 152-run study organized and reproducible, and reporting results to my advisor with their limits and not just the good news.

Research collaboration · UFRGS · since 2026

4-bit Winograd on FPGA: four products per DSP, 1.98× the 8-bit throughput

3,379 GOPS on VGG16 with 1,024 DSPs (ZCU104) · the 4-bit network I trained: 68.51% ImageNet top-1, 3.08 points below float

Winograd FPGA Accelerators — with Thomas Schmitz ↗

Verilog accelerators for 3×3 convolution with the Winograd algorithm (F(2,3), F(4,3), F(6,3); INT8 and 4-bit), designed by my labmate Thomas Schmitz; I bring the network and accuracy side. Co-author of an invited IEEE OJCAS article extending Thomas’s SBCCI 2026 paper (in preparation) and of an ISCAS paper under review.

Contribution & learnings

How I contributeI evaluate Thomas’s accelerator lines on 16 networks adapted to 3×3 kernels on an AMD Alveo U200: cycle counts from netlist simulation, Fmax and power from Vivado, and accuracy under the accelerator’s own arithmetic. For the OJCAS article I trained the 4-bit network directly in the Winograd domain (per-filter scales, three seeds), took it into the RTL with bias, and revised the paper’s numbers and text.

What I’m learningAn arithmetic gain is a ceiling, not throughput: bigger tiles need wider data and harder timing closure, and hardware numbers only count once they match a software reference bit for bit. Working on someone else’s design taught me to improve it without touching it: measure through his test gate, report what breaks, and let the designer decide.

Projeto VORIS ↗

Laser-triangulation code ↗Research report (PDF, PT) ↗

VORIS (Remotely Operated Vehicle for Underwater Inspection) is a FINEP-funded project at UFSC’s Metrology and Automation Laboratory (LABMETRO), with Petrobras as an interested party: an ROV with 3D optical measurement systems for submerged structures. In my undergraduate research, working with Prof. Diego Fabre, I built and evaluated its laser-triangulation testbed: a camera, a laser and a rotating mirror that sweep a scene and turn it into a 3D point cloud.

Contribution & learnings

How I contributedI built the system end to end: the hardware (an Arduino Uno R4 driving a stepper-motor mirror read by an absolute encoder, PWM laser control and image capture synchronized to the scan angle), subpixel laser-line extraction with an intensity-weighted centroid (far less noise than thresholding), the geometric triangulation model (camera normalization, camera–laser geometry and mirror-angle correction), a gradient-boosted regression model trained on labeled scans to correct systematic depth errors, and a graphical interface to run it all.

What I learnedA model is only as good as its calibration: geometry was accurate near the center of the view, but small errors in calibration and laser angle grew toward the edges. Learning those errors from data reduced the artifacts there, but only as far as the training data reached, which points to a hybrid with geometry as the backbone. Working at LABMETRO also put me, an electrical-engineering student, in a mechanical-engineering lab alongside metrologists and computer-vision researchers, working with professors from different specialties: I learned to explain my subsystem in each of their terms, to turn their critiques into improvements to the testbed, and to deliver on a funded project’s schedule.

UFRGS’s Model United Nations conference. As the delegate of Israel at the UN Office of Counter-Terrorism (UNOCT), I debated terrorism in the Sahel and cyberterrorism in English, negotiating with delegations whose positions were often far from mine.

Contribution & learnings

How I contributedTo prepare, I followed the news on cyberterrorism, cybersecurity and the Sahel closely. The preparation also became a habit: I keep up with cybersecurity and geopolitics day to day, from new attacks and vulnerabilities to regional conflicts and the security debates around them, because an argument only holds when it reflects the current picture.
